## Cross-Cutting Findings

**Fixing the data doesn't fix the framing.** This session had three distinct passes on the same underlying problem — accurate content (P6), accurate links (P7), accurate *language about the source of truth* (P8) — and each one only became visible after the previous one was done. Worth remembering for future correction work: check not just "is the fact right" but "does everything downstream of the fact (links, prose, credits) still assume the old framing."

**The P5 completeness-audit lesson generalized cleanly.** Session 7 discovered that "checking for wrong" and "checking for missing" are different audits; P6 applied that lesson across all 50 states from the start rather than needing a second pass.

**IAFF is a real, repeatedly-wrong dependency, not a hypothetical rigor concern.** Across all 50 states, this session found concrete, citable IAFF errors — wrong statutes, wrong dates, omitted conditions, mischaracterized benefit types, and at least one fabricated claim (NM) that had propagated into `bob.md` and the JSON. This validates the decision to de-source the dashboard from IAFF entirely rather than treating it as "mostly right, spot-check as needed."
