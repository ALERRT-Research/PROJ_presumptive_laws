## Cross-Cutting Findings (Session 6 Research Intelligence)

**Non-WC states confirmed (9 total):** GA, NC, IN, KY, AL, MA, IA, AR, SD. None have workers' compensation presumptions — all operate through pension, insurance benefit, or LODD programs. The dashboard benefit-type field is load-bearing for usability.

**Cancer standard taxonomy (three categories):**
1. **IARC/NTP dynamic** — auto-expands with science; IL, PA, NY, NJ, OH, WA (pension), WY, MT, NV, LA, WI, KS
2. **Enumerated list** — requires legislation to expand; TX, FL, AZ, TN, NC, MD, VA, MN, MO, SC, VT, ME, NH (all types = special case)
3. **Exposure-linkage** — individual carcinogen proof required per claimant; ND, AR, AK. Dashboard needs a third coding option.

**Strongest cancer standard:** Rhode Island — conclusive presumption, all cancer types, no rebuttal possible. Covers paid and volunteer FF.

**Most misleading IAFF page:** Oklahoma § 85A-14 is presented as a presumptive benefit; it is the explicit statutory barrier to WC coverage for cardiac events, imposing a higher burden than general negligence.

**IAFF omission pattern (systematic):** AK PTSD (2025), ID PTSD (2023), CT PTSD (2021), ME LE cardiovascular (2023), TN PTSD (§ 7-51-206, 2023) — all real laws affecting real claimants, all invisible on the IAFF page.

**Rhode Island structure:** Operates through IOD salary-continuation + accidental disability retirement (Title 45), independent of Title 28 WC. Presumptions shift burden within pension/IOD framework.

**South Dakota's opt-in gap:** § 45-19-16 respiratory statute requires individual municipalities to pass enabling ordinances — coverage is not statewide, and a statewide checkmark on the dashboard would mislead users.
