## Fallen Heroes Act / LODD education gap

IAFF runs strong education and outreach helping firefighter families understand and apply for line-of-duty-death (LODD) benefits, including state-level representatives who help families navigate the process directly. Law enforcement families largely lack an equivalent — many don't even know they're eligible for LODD benefits in the first place.

Peter flagged this as a real, substantive gap. **Not yet clear where ALERRT fits into addressing it** — this is an open question, not a commitment, and needs more thought before it becomes a project.

## FOP (Fraternal Order of Police) — major opportunity

Vickie has been in conversation with FOP contacts in Pennsylvania. The Pennsylvania FOP president has floated giving her **10 minutes to speak at the FOP national meeting**.

Why this matters: FOP's outreach covers the large majority of working law enforcement nationally. Peter flagged FOP as probably the single best-positioned partner for (a) research funding and (b) fielding a national LE health/cancer survey. Worth watching closely — if the national-meeting slot happens, that's a direct line to a national LE audience.

## City of Hope advisory board appointment

Vickie has accepted a **5-year appointment to City of Hope's advisory board**, and has explicitly named law enforcement cancer screening/diagnosis/treatment as her stated priority for that role.

City of Hope is described as the largest cancer hospital north of Texas — a major western/national cancer center. This gives Vickie (and by extension, this partnership) a serious institutional foothold for LE-cancer-focused advocacy, separate from and complementary to the FOP angle.

## Washington State — no LE cancer presumptive law

Per Vickie, Washington state has no cancer presumptive law covering law enforcement. This is consistent with the project's own WA research: RCW 51.32.185 covers firefighters and EMTs for cancer but explicitly excludes LE.

A Washington officer recently died of cancer, leaving a family behind, and there is a push underway to pass a bill named in his honor. Peter wants to flag: **any way ALERRT's research/data could support that legislative push would be valuable.**

**Needs follow-up:** identify the specific bill and sponsor once the bill exists/is introduced — nothing concrete to track yet.

## California PPE / breathing-apparatus bill

A major California law enforcement union is pushing to lobby for a bill mandating PPE — specifically respiratory protection/breathing apparatus — for law enforcement officers.

Rationale: LEOs routinely respond to fire scenes to cordon off the area and are exposed to toxic smoke/fumes, but unlike firefighters — who have sophisticated standard-issue breathing apparatus — LEOs currently have no standard-issue respiratory PPE at all. Framed as a significant, currently-unaddressed occupational exposure gap, and a potential angle distinct from the presumptive-law/WC-benefits threads above.
