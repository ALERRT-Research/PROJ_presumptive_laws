## Cross-Cutting Findings (Research Intelligence)

**Non-WC states:** Georgia and North Carolina have no workers' compensation presumptions. Both pivoted to employer-benefit insurance programs after WC legislation failed (GA vetoed 2016; NC Senate repeatedly blocked). The dashboard needs language distinguishing "presumption" (burden-shifting) from "employer benefit program" (guaranteed payment, no burden shift).

**Indiana exceptionalism:** Municipal firefighters and police are explicitly excluded from Indiana's general WC occupational disease system (IC 22-3-7-2). Indiana's "presumption" is a pension board determination, not a WC claim. Denial appeals go to INPRS, not the Workers' Compensation Board. Functionally different in ways that matter to claimants.

**PTSD is the live legislative issue:** Michigan (HB 5952/5953, 2026), North Carolina (HB 463, 2025), Illinois (HB 3529, 2023 failed), Virginia (2026 SB812 pending), Arizona (HB 2204, 2026 pending). Multiple major states in active legislative cycle simultaneously.

**Tennessee IAFF quality issue:** The IAFF page directs users to § 8-50-119 (10 mandatory counseling visits — a nice thing, not a presumption) while the actual WC PTSD presumption (§ 7-51-206, James Samples Act, enacted 2023, effective Jan 2024) is completely absent from the page. This is the clearest IAFF data quality failure found so far and affects firefighter families making real coverage decisions.

**IARC vs. enumerated list split:** States using IARC dynamic standard (IN, IL, PA, NY, NJ, OH, WA pension track): cancer list expands automatically as science evolves. States using enumerated lists (TX, FL, AZ, TN, NC, MD, VA): expansion requires legislation. Dashboard should surface this distinction.
