# Idea Brief: Tracking the Loosening of Cancer Presumption Standards
**Prepared for:** meeting with Vickie Speed (CEO, Blue Cancer Connect), week of 2026-08-11
**Prepared:** 2026-08-07 (updated same day post-QC) | **Status:** pitch-stage idea, not yet a funded project

## 1. The idea

States defining a firefighter/officer cancer presumption don't all define "qualifying cancer" the same way, and many have changed that definition over time — moving from a short enumerated list to a broader list, a dynamic IARC-carcinogen standard, or an "any cancer" standard. Each transition is a natural experiment: a state expands *who counts* without changing *how much* benefits pay — exactly the coverage-gap mechanism BCC cares about. A fifty-state timeline of when and how each state loosened (or, in two confirmed cases, tightened) its standard lets us test what predicts loosening — litigation losses, union lobbying, denied-claim publicity — and gives BCC a ready-made advocacy map of which states are overdue and what precedent already exists. **All 50 states are now independently researched and QC-gated (scores 97–100/100); what follows is the verified, citable set, not a draft.**

## 2. Candidate states — QC-verified 2026-08-07

### CONFIRMED — precise mechanism + date, ready to cite

| State | Prior standard | New standard | Act / date | How confirmed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New Hampshire | IARC carcinogen-linkage gate (added by SB541, 2018) | Gate struck entirely — unrestricted "all types of cancer" | SB71, Ch. 206:1, eff. 2023-08-04 | Direct redline/bill-text comparison. Strongest, most precisely documented case in the dataset. |
| Iowa | Closed 14-type enumerated list (2009) | Struck and replaced with open-ended generic "abnormal cell growth" clinical definition — any cancer | HF 969, 2025 Iowa Acts ch. 146, eff. 2025-07-01 | Direct pre/post statutory text comparison. Pension-only system (MFPRSI/PORS/IPERS), no WC track. |
| Louisiana | Enumerated list + pre-existing IARC clause (RS 33:2011) | "Statistically significant increased risk" catch-all added on top; also dropped the "disabling" requirement | Act 287/SB 63, 2017 | Enrolled-bill redline — new (underscored) text confirmed distinct from the untouched IARC clause. |

Also still high-confidence (smaller expansions, reserve material): Arizona, New Mexico, Maryland, Utah, Virginia, Washington, Tennessee, West Virginia, Rhode Island. Two corrections: Colorado's SB26-184 expansion was **vetoed** June 2026 (current law unchanged); Kentucky's HB34 **died in Senate** — neither is enacted law.

### STILL UNRESOLVED — real leads, not yet nailed down

- **Massachusetts** — cancer standard's DPH-commissioner catch-all (hybrid IARC-plus-organ-system) is present by 1990, but whether it originates in the 1990 act or a later amendment (2018 is confirmed only for a *separate* addition — breast/reproductive-organ cancers) is unresolved; malegislature.gov/PERAC blocked automated access this round.
- **North Carolina** — the pilot-program-era cancer standard (uncodified 2021–22, retroactive to 1/1/2022) that preceded the current Article 86A IARC standard could not be located despite extensive attempts. Top open item in the whole dataset.
- **Louisiana (secondary)** — separate from the confirmed 2017 catch-all above, whether the *original* IARC-carcinogen clause was part of the 1995 enactment or added by a 2004 amendment remains unconfirmed (best-evidence guess points to 2004; pre-2006 LA bill archives aren't text-searchable).
- **California** — now confirmed: the IARC-exposure cancer standard (Lab. Code § 3212.1) was enacted in the 1999–2000 session, retroactive to claims filed on/after 1/1/1997 (renamed the "William Dallas Jones Cancer Presumption Act" in 2010). No evidence found of a narrower predecessor standard — exact bill/chapter number still unconfirmed, but this now looks like a broad-from-inception case, not a loosening event.
- **Oklahoma** — pension cancer standard is unrestricted ("any cancer," no enumeration), but the year it became unrestricted is unconfirmed; may have been unrestricted from inception rather than loosened. Useful as a documented negative/non-case.
- **Illinois** — WC track's "any cancer" standard (no IARC gate) may be the broadest in the entire dataset, but it appears original to the statute, not a later loosening — same from-inception caveat as Oklahoma.

### COUNTER-EXAMPLES — tightening, useful as a falsification check

- **Texas** — SB 2551 (2019) moved *away* from an IARC-exposure standard *to* a fixed 11-type enumerated list. A genuine tightening, confirmed via independent rerun (QC 97/100).
- **Nevada** — SB 153 (2015) narrowed cancer, heart, lung, and both hepatitis presumptions by limiting eligibility to persons actively employed or volunteering *at the time of diagnosis* (closing a broader post-separation window). Confirmed via direct bill text; a genuine, differently-shaped tightening than Texas's (eligibility window vs. standard type) — useful for showing the mechanism runs both directions in more than one way.

## 3. Connection to the LEO-vs-Firefighter backlog idea

The backlog project ("LEO vs. Firefighter Mortality and the Presumptive Law Coverage Gap," added 2026-07-03) argues comparable mortality burdens across occupations haven't produced comparable coverage — firefighters got there through decades of lobbying, LEOs haven't. This idea turns the same policy-responsiveness argument sideways: instead of comparing occupations at a point in time, it tracks standard strictness within firefighter cancer coverage over time. Together they argue coverage generosity tracks organizing capacity and litigation pressure more than actual risk — one paper on the gap between occupations, one on the gap closing unevenly even within the best-covered occupation.

## 4. What's next

Basic re-verification is done — all 50 states are independently researched and QC-gated as of 2026-08-07 (97–100/100, all PASS). The remaining work is no longer "confirm the data is real," it's targeted deep digging on the two genuinely unresolved cases: **North Carolina** (pre-2024 pilot-program cancer standard — the top open item in the dataset) and **Massachusetts** (DPH-commissioner catch-all's origin date). Both have hit dead ends on standard search/fetch methods (blocked sites, non-indexed archives) and likely need a records request, Westlaw/Lexis historical annotations, or a library-assisted archive pull rather than another automated pass. Secondary, lower-priority: Louisiana's 1995-vs-2004 IARC-clause dating and California's exact 1999–2000 bill/chapter number — neither blocks the pitch, since both states' *headline* findings (LA's 2017 catch-all; CA's broad-from-inception status) are already confirmed.

**Project shape, if pursued:** a state-year panel coding cancer-standard breadth (enumerated count, IARC/any-cancer flag) alongside union density, litigation events, and sponsorship data; event-study/staggered-DiD around each loosening date testing whether denied-claim lawsuits or union campaigns precede expansions, with Texas's 2019 and Nevada's 2015 narrowings as falsification checks on "coverage only ratchets one way."
